Fuel demand shedding with original delivery capacities
=====================================================

The campaign preserves optimized base-network biomass import delivery Links
(import_capacity_MW: null) and disables the former doubling of fuel-accounting
Links (fuel_backup.enable: false). Existing restoration logic removes legacy
parallel backup Links and reverses recorded delivery-capacity increases.
Prepared and solved outputs must be regenerated before comparing results.
The 2035 interconnector overlays inherit these settings from the main overlay.

Enable fuel-demand shedding under disruption.load_shedding.fuel. A separate
Generator is attached to each positive actual fuel Load, not indiscriminately
to primary fuel supply buses. Its hourly maximum equals that Load's positive
consumption profile after donor-weather transfer. Repeating preparation
refreshes these limits and does not duplicate assets. Setting enable: false
removes these Generators. No fuel-consuming CHP or boiler is given a separate
fuel slack: unmet electricity/heat is handled at its final-demand buses.

Built-in demand classifications cover biomass, biogas, gas, oil (including
naphtha, aviation kerosene, shipping, road transport and agriculture), coal,
lignite, methanol, hydrogen and ammonia. Only actual positive Loads generate
slack assets; empty fuel-supply buses, storage inventories, process emissions
and electricity/heat loads do not acquire extra fuel supply. Electricity and
heat shedding continue to use their existing preparation rules.

Prices are EUR/MWh of unmet final fuel demand. Default: 23300. This is an
uncalibrated modelling assumption, not an empirically established fuel VoLL.
The precedence is costs_by_load > costs_by_carrier (the demand BUS carrier)
> costs_by_fuel > default_cost, followed by a regional_cost_adders adjustment
using the exact bus location. Prices must be positive and finite; regional
adders may be negative if the final price stays positive. Unknown setting keys
and nonexistent individual Load names fail validation.

Example configuration:

disruption:
  load_shedding:
    fuel:
      enable: true
      default_cost: 23300
      costs_by_fuel: {biomass: 30000, gas: 25000, oil: 23300}
      costs_by_carrier: {solid biomass for industry: 32000}
      costs_by_load: {DK0 0AC solid biomass for industry: 35000}
      regional_cost_adders: {DK0 0AC: 100}
      demand_carriers: {}  # Additional exact bus-carrier -> fuel mappings.

Each solved case writes:
- fuel_shedding_by_demand.csv: demand, unserved and served MWh, fraction
  unserved, peak MW, shedding hours, penalty cost, and demand/fuel/region labels.
- fuel_shedding_by_fuel.csv: whole-network totals by fuel. Peak and hours use
  the simultaneous aggregate profile, rather than summing asset peaks/hours.
- fuel_shedding_timeseries.csv: hourly unmet MW per shedding Generator; the
  by-demand table maps Generator names to Loads and regions.

dispatch_summary.csv also contains fuel_unserved_MWh and fuel_shedding_cost_EUR.
Energy and hours use generator snapshot weights; penalty costs use objective
weights. Reports use only saved implemented snapshots after rolling dispatch,
so overlap/lookahead solutions are not double counted. Fuel shedding costs
are also excluded from the heatmap's dispatch-cost-excluding-shedding metric.

Interpretation: shedding is unmet prescribed fuel demand, not an actual fuel
purchase. No fuel consumption emissions are added for shed demand. It can
reflect an upstream resource shortage OR a binding original delivery Link.
Comparisons should subtract the matched no-outage baseline and inspect fuel
budgets and delivery flows before attributing the cause. Shared fuel buses
(e.g. industrial hydrogen) permit rationing between local demand and other
uses; slack remains capped at the actual prescribed demand.

Existing annual resource budgets, reference scarcity prices, physical storage
and opening-inventory assumptions are retained. These changes do not launch
production solves or overwrite saved base networks/results.
